Why the obvious fix would have been wrong
The tempting move was “show the fee-wallet balance as the LI.FI slice”. That would have double-counted CCTP: its $6.91 is already recorded in the database AND sitting in those same wallets. Attributing the whole $7.99 balance to LI.FI would have inflated total revenue by nearly the entire CCTP figure. I checked the CCTP quote route before writing any SQL — this is exactly the kind of thing that silently corrupts a money dashboard.

2 · The rule the corrected queries follow

Two independent measurements exist for the shared integrator pot — what we recorded per swap, and what the wallets hold. Neither is complete on its own: recording only starts with doc 123, and a wallet balance drops the day you withdraw it. So the queries take GREATEST(recorded, wallet balance) for that pot, and add the self-charged fees on top:

total = savings + soroswap                       (recorded, ours)
      + GREATEST(cctp + lifi recorded, fee wallets)   (the shared pot)

This double-counts nothing, undercounts nothing, and self-corrects: every new LI.FI swap raises the recorded side and the wallet side by the same amount, and if you ever withdraw the wallets, the recorded figure takes over as the floor.
