The Swap Failure Atlas

Doc 113 — every swap type, every step, and exactly what happens when each step fails. Written from the code as it stands on feat/swap-matrix-hardening (2026-08-28), each claim verified at its source, not from memory.

1 · Your two questions, answered straight

“If something fails, does the refund begin automatically?”

Where a refund is the right answer — yes. But the honest, complete answer is that most failures do not need a refund at all, because of one design rule: funds travel via your own addresses at every step. Money is never sitting with us or with a counterparty waiting to be given back — so “recovery” usually means either “nothing ever moved” or “finish the journey forward”. The full truth table:

situationwhat happensautomatic?
A step fails before anything is signed (guards, quotes, fees) Nothing moved. You fix the input or just retry. NOTHING TO REFUND
The source transaction reverts on-chain (inbound swap) The chain undid everything — funds never left (minus network fee). Calm grey ending; the record retires itself. NOTHING TO REFUND
A bridge leg fails mid-route on a LI.FI swap The bridge protocol itself returns the funds to your source address. We track it and tell you: “your {sym} was returned — no funds were lost.” AUTOMATIC (by the bridge)
Outbound swap: your USDC reached Base, but the final exchange keeps getting rejected on-chain Two automatic attempts first (original route, then a re-route excluding the failed bridge and DEX). If both revert: the refund starts itself — the popup stays open, says “The exchange route kept failing — bringing your USDC back automatically”, and walks the refund steps. AUTOMATIC — zero prompts with autopilot on; exactly one passkey confirmation without it (the refund is itself an on-chain transaction, and only autopilot may sign for you)
Any failure where retrying is genuinely viable (a declined signature, a network blip) Red panel with Try again / the banner’s one-tap recovery. Auto-refunding here would throw away a swap that one tap can finish. ONE TAP, YOUR CHOICE

“Are the errors user-friendly now?”

Yes, with one honest caveat. What is now true everywhere:

  • Raw chain errors never reach you: a single tested classifier translates Horizon/Soroban codes, passkey/WebAuthn outcomes, nonce races, rate limits and timeouts into plain sentences; anything that still looks technical (stack traces, hex dumps) collapses to a calm generic line instead of leaking.
  • No more double-translation: the collision that rewrote “not enough ETH for the network fee” into “the network didn’t answer — try again” is gone and pinned by a test.
  • Endings are color-honest: grey = nothing moved, nothing to do (calm panel, Close only); red = your money moved and needs a decision (Try again / Bring back). Red is no longer used to say “nothing happened”.
  • Surfaces cannot contradict each other about facts: the LI.FI verdict, the banner phase table and the failure copy each live in one tested module.

The caveat: a truly unknown error (something no pattern matches, under 200 characters, not technical-looking) is shown as-is. That is deliberate — better an odd but genuine sentence than a wrong translation — but it means “friendly” is guaranteed by classes, not by enumerating every possible string.

2 · The map: which swap uses which machine

pairenginesignatures

BTC, ETH and SOL share each engine’s code path — the per-asset differences are the signing method (BTC = PSBT raw-payload signing with a spend cap; ETH = EIP-1559 with a live gas reserve; SOL = rent-exempt minimum guard) and the settlement clock. A failure behaves the same way for all three unless a row below says otherwise.

3 · Soroswap: XLM ↔ USDC

The simplest machine, and the reason it needs no refunds: the whole swap is one atomic Stellar transaction. It either applies completely or does not apply at all — there is no half-way state to rescue.

Journey: source chain → (LI.FI) → USDC lands at your own Base address → (Circle burn+mint) → your Stellar wallet. The design consequence: once the source leg succeeds, recovery is always forward — the USDC is already yours on Base and only needs to finish the trip.

5 · CCTP outbound: USDC → BTC / ETH / SOL

Journey: (optional funding move from Lobstr) → Stellar burn → (Circle) → USDC minted at your own Base address → gas top-up → LI.FI pivot to the target chain → delivery. This is the one flow with a real refund story, because after the bridge your USDC sits on Base waiting to be exchanged — if the exchange cannot happen, the right move is to bring it home.

The one hand-off to support — named honestly
If the pivot transaction confirms but LI.FI then reports the final leg as failed or refunded mid-delivery (extremely rare — the route already accepted the funds), the popup says plainly: your USDC is at your own Base address, untouched by anyone else — contact support. It is the one place we currently stop rather than automate, because the money’s exact position depends on the individual route. Your funds are never in limbo — always at your own address.

7 · The rules that make all of the above true

ruleconsequence
Funds travel via your own addresses at every step No failure can strand money with us or a counterparty; recovery is always “finish forward” or “bring home”, both from addresses you control.
Record before broadcast; never sign what we didn’t verify No transaction can happen invisibly, and no transaction can spend more than its quote said.
Broadcasts are idempotent; every money path has a second provider A lost response or a dead RPC/relay/Horizon degrades to a retry, never a double-spend or a blank screen.
Each transfer moves only its own share Two simultaneous swaps can no longer eat each other’s funds.
Failed routes are excluded per-retry, never removed globally Failover is automatic and cannot ping-pong onto a route that already reverted; the bridge stays available for everyone else.
Grey = nothing moved · Red = needs your decision · Banner = one-tap forward recovery · Refund = starts itself when options are exhausted The color and the buttons always tell you the true state and the true next step.
Terminal is terminalA dead record retires itself everywhere at once — popup, banner, activity — and can never come back as a ghost.

8 · Honest gaps that remain

  • The support hand-off in §5 — post-pivot delivery failure is surfaced honestly but not yet automated.
  • The refund burn needs one passkey without autopilot. Unavoidable: it is an on-chain transaction from your address, and only the autopilot policy you explicitly enable may sign in your place.
  • Bridge-protocol refunds run on the bridge’s clock. We report them truthfully the moment they are visible, but we cannot accelerate them.
  • A closed tab in the final seconds of delivery records the quoted (minimum) amount instead of the exact delivered figure — cosmetic, and the run-page work (doc 93 phase 0c) removes it.
  • Unknown errors pass through as-is when they read like a human sentence — by design, but worth restating.
