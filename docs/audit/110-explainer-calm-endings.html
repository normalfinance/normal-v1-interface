Calm Endings

Branch feat/swap-matrix-hardening, run 15 — a swap that moved nothing must end quietly, truthfully, and clean itself up.

1 · What this run is for

Your ETH → USDC swap reverted on the blockchain itself (verified: receipt status 0x0, LI.FI status FAILED). Your ETH never left; only ~$0.13 of gas was spent. The app’s handling of that ending was the problem: a scary red box, a Try again button routed into the wrong recovery, and a banner that kept advertising the dead swap as “money in flight” forever. Your instruction: honest message, no red, no Try again, banner gone. That is exactly what shipped.

2 · The new ending, surface by surface

The popup: a quiet grey panel instead of a red alarm

A swap whose funds provably never moved is now a calm ending, not an error. The failed step gets a small grey marker (not the red triangle), and below the steps sits a soft grey panel — no red anywhere — with this copy:

The exchange rejected this swap on-chain — the transaction reverted, which
usually means the price moved past its protection while it was being
processed. Your ETH never left your wallet; only the small network fee was
spent. Get a fresh quote and swap again whenever you like.

That is the honest “because of what”: the route’s own on-chain protection (slippage/deadline) rejected the trade, the revert undid everything, and the only cost was gas. One button: Close. No Try again — there is nothing to retry; the right next step is a fresh quote, and the card is right there. The bridge-refund ending (“funds returned to your wallet”) gets the same calm treatment.

Red still exists — where it belongs
The red surface stays for failures that need something from you (a mid-bridge halt with funds on Base, a declined signature). The dividing line is simple: red = your money moved and needs an action; grey = nothing moved, nothing to do.

The banner: ghosts remove themselves

The dead row stayed on the banner because its retirement was fire-and-forget. Now the state machine itself retires it: every advance pass — the banner’s 30-second poke and the cron — checks a CREATED inbound row’s source transaction against LI.FI, and if LI.FI says it failed or was refunded, the row is marked FAILED with an honest detail (“Source transaction reverted on-chain — funds never left the sender; nothing was bridged”) and drops off the banner. Your currently stuck row will clear itself within about 30 seconds of the page being open — no clicks needed.

If someone does click “Receive USDC” on a ghost

The recovery used to find 0 USDC and throw “No USDC found on Base — it may already be bridging” — wrong, and a dead end. Now, on finding nothing, it asks the server to verify the source leg against LI.FI right there: a dead leg is retired on the spot with an honest note (“that swap had already failed on-chain — nothing was bridged and your funds never left your wallet”), and a genuinely-still-bridging leg gets a patient “still working, nothing to do” — both as info toasts, not errors.

One verdict, shared

Three surfaces used to interpret LI.FI’s status with three private copies of the logic — which is exactly how they ended up telling you three different stories. The reading now lives in one tested module (lib/cctp/lifi-verdict.ts, 4 tests, one of them your exact transaction’s response), used by the engine, the server route and the state machine. The surfaces can still word things differently; they can no longer disagree about what happened.

3 · Honest limits

  • The copy says “usually means the price moved past its protection” because the revert reason is not in the receipt without a trace — slippage/deadline is the common cause, and the copy claims no more than we know.
  • Outbound failures (money already on Base) keep their existing red flow with Bring back — those genuinely need the user, so they are not calm endings.
