Round 2 Explainer

What shipped on 2026-08-26 across feat/better-ramps and feat/honest-errors (wave 1), explained from the user's chair: what used to happen, what actually went wrong underneath, and what happens now. The manual test steps live in doc 91 — QA Round 2 (R25–R32); the full error audit and the remaining waves live in 90-honest-errors-audit.md.

1 · Swaps that heal themselves — failure classes + bridge/DEX failover (QA R25, R26)

Before

If you swapped USDC → SOL and the exchange leg failed on-chain, the app treated it like a signing problem: it asked for your biometrics again, you signed the exact same doomed transaction, it failed identically — and the error box showed a raw hex dump.

What was actually wrong (the forensic)

We replayed every failed transaction at its historic block. All of them — across two different bridges (Mayan and Relay) — died at the same address: a fake, unverified "Tesla TSLA" token whose transfer() always reverts, embedded inside LI.FI's DEX pre-swap step (tool "fly"). The bridges were never broken. That is why "try again" could never work: every fresh quote included the same poisoned swap path.

Proven live, same day
15:27:55 swap started → 15:28:47 Mayan+fly route reverted → the app silently retried with that bridge and that DEX excluded → 15:28:53 a clean route (NEAR) succeeded → SOL delivered. Six seconds, zero biometric prompts, zero clicks.

Now

  • The server tells the app why a step failed. An on-chain revert never falls back to a passkey prompt — your signature cannot fix a route the chain already rejected.
  • On a revert, the app retries once automatically with the failed bridge and the failed DEX step excluded from the quote (denyBridges + denyExchanges). Nothing is ever removed from routing in general — the exclusion lives only inside the retry of the transfer that just failed.
  • The row records "via mayan through fly (0x…)", so later retries from the banner exclude everything that already failed. Exclusions accumulate — no ping-pong between two broken routes.
  • If everything fails: an honest popup ("The exchange route failed on the provider side — your USDC is safe in your own account") with two working buttons — Try again and Bring back as USDC.

2 · Sends that cannot strand dust — exact MAX for every asset (QA R27, R28)

Before

MAX-sending all your SOL failed with "The network rejected this send." Cause: the MAX button rounded a 9-decimal asset to 8 decimals, stranding 9 lamports — and Solana forbids leaving an account with more than zero but less than ~0.00089 SOL (InsufficientFundsForRent, simulation-verified). A second rounding in the review screen (7 decimals for everything non-BTC) could re-create the dust even after a fix. And BTC MAX was a guaranteed failure: it offered your full balance, which can never cover amount + fee.

Now

AssetBehavior
SOLMAX computes in integer lamports from a live balance read and leaves exactly 0 (allowed). A typed amount that would leave forbidden dust is blocked before the passkey prompt: "leave at least 0.001 SOL".
BTCMAX = all UTXOs minus the real sweep fee (the same vbyte math the builder uses). Sub-546-sat sends are blocked pre-passkey (the network rejects dust outputs).
Stellar USDCThe fee is paid in XLM — a sender at the reserve floor is warned before signing instead of failing at Horizon.
ETH / XLMAudited: already correct (live gas estimate + reserve math). Unchanged.
Rule this created
MAX is computed in the asset's own integer units from a LIVE read — display-rounded MAX math will strand dust on some chain, every time. Never collect a signature for a transaction that must fail.

3 · Money in flight you can trust (QA R29)

Before

"Buying SOL on Coinbase" messages doubled up and never went away — even after the SOL arrived. Three compounding causes: the arrival baseline was only captured for Stellar assets, the arrival check skipped every non-Stellar chain, and the 45-minute cleanup lived only in a scheduled job that never runs outside production. A state machine whose only exit is a cron that doesn't run has no exits.

Now

  • The indicator lives inside the balance card, right under the number it explains — a compact blue "on the way" chip, for every asset. Failures keep the dismissible banner above Activity.
  • The server captures a live baseline for every chain at purchase time, and re-opening a checkout reuses the active row instead of stacking a duplicate.
  • Arrival is proven by the chain — balance above baseline, or a confirmed incoming transfer newer than the row — checked both by the open tab (within ~10 s) and by the server on every read, so the chip clears even if no tab was watching when the money landed.
  • Abandoned checkouts clear themselves after 45 minutes on the next page load. No cron needed.

4 · Activity rows that tell the truth about wallets (QA R30)

Before: with Lobstr connected, SOL/ETH/BTC rows were tagged "Lobstr" — impossible, since external wallets are Stellar-only and cannot sign those transactions. Now: a row that declares its funding source keeps that truth (a swap Lobstr paid for still says Lobstr), and every other native-chain row is tagged Normal wallet, derived from the chain registry — no hardcoded lists.

5 · Honest errors, wave 1 — the infrastructure (QA R31, R32)

A four-sweep audit found ~102 places where failures reached users as raw machine text, silently vanished, or blamed the wrong thing. Wave 1 shipped the plumbing the rest rides on:

One classifier for every error surface

Every toast and failure box now funnels through friendlyAppError(). If a raw Stellar code, Turnkey machine string, viem race, revert, rate limit, or timeout reaches it → you see one plain-English sentence. If the text is already human (like MoneyGram's useful "below minimum" rejection) → it passes through untouched. If it's a JSON dump, stack trace, hex blob, or internal config name → it collapses to a neutral line, and the raw detail stays in the console for support. Eighteen surfaces converted; twelve jest cases pin the behavior.

You used to seeYou now see
Turnkey error 16: credential ID could not be found… organizationId=… "Your device signed with a passkey from a different account — reload the page and try again."
Transaction failed: tx_insufficient_balance "Not enough XLM to cover the network fee — add a little XLM and try again."
nonce too low "The network was mid-update — try again, it usually works immediately."
deposit_for_burn submit failed: {"_attributes":{…}} "That didn't go through. Please try again in a moment."
challenge failed: 401 Unauthorized The one session banner (below) — after a silent retry.

One session-expired story

Any authed request that hits 401 now silently refreshes your session and retries once. Only if that still fails: a single black banner at the top — "Your session expired — please sign in again" — instead of a different cryptic failure on every surface. The money-in-flight banner keeps its last data during an outage instead of pretending nothing is pending.

Nothing internal leaks

Server responses no longer carry stack traces, internal MoneyGram hosts, request payloads, or 2 KB HTML dumps — that detail stays in server logs. The anchor's genuinely useful messages now reach you; the diagnostics never do.

One flaky node can't lie to you

Money-moving legs (the Base pivot, arrival polling, swap-delivery confirmation) now try a list of RPC providers instead of one public node. The worst case this kills: an RPC outage used to be reported as "Cross-chain swap failed — no funds were moved" while your funds had moved.

7 · Honest errors, wave 2 — money-path truth (QA R33–R36)

The dangerous silents: places where the app either lost track of money-state or told you something false about your money. Every one now either retries, blocks, or says so:

  • Money-state writes never vanish — recording a burn/swap hash retries three times with backoff; if it still fails you're told to keep the tab open (instead of the transfer becoming invisible to both the cron and the recovery banner). A timed-out Stellar burn hands its hash to the row anyway — the transaction may still land.
  • No more eternal spinners — a Soroswap swap with an expired wallet session shows "reconnect and try again" instead of "Preparing the swap" forever; a slow bridge (>20 min) says "still on its way — track it in Activity" instead of spinning silently.
  • Guards fail visible — if the memo check can't run, Send shows an amber "we could not verify whether this address needs a memo" warning instead of silently passing (that guard exists because of a real lost-funds incident); the phrase-export dialog blocks with Retry when the wallet list won't load, instead of possibly exporting the wrong wallet's phrase.
  • Outage ≠ absence — a failed Coinbase status check warns "could not reach Coinbase" instead of announcing "No pending cash-out" after a real sale, and the cash-out resume now survives a page refresh.
  • Small dignities — closing the wallet picker says "cancelled" instead of nothing; the MoneyGram details button can no longer die silently; its polls time out honestly and re-authenticate after session expiry; a transient wallet-info timeout no longer claims "Turnkey wallet not found".

8 · Honest errors, wave 3 — unknown is never zero (QA R37–R39)

The worst error message in existence is money that looks gone. Wave 3 makes every outage render as an outage:

  • Portfolio — a failed balance load shows "Couldn't load your balances — Retry", never "Total portfolio value $0.00 / No holdings yet". BTC gained the same failure surface the other chains had; the companion Normal wallet keeps a last-good copy so one Horizon blip can't erase it everywhere.
  • Prices — a CoinGecko outage serves the last-good prices (up to a week old) instead of valuing real holdings at $0; charts serve their older series instead of going blank; a truly unknown price renders as "—".
  • Savings — "we don't know" became its own state: a Horizon blip no longer nags a set-up account to "Activate", never silently disarms the XLM fee guard, and never auto-opens setup; stats say "Unavailable — retrying…" instead of skeleton-forever; the earnings chart and history say they failed instead of drawing $0.00.
  • The DeFindex 429 bursts (the oldest open backlog item) — every call now rides a shared gate: two at a time, 300 ms apart, jittered retries. The cold-load burst that tripped their limit every time is gone. The gate's tests caught a real concurrency race while being written.

9 · What's next

  • Wave 3 — unknown ≠ zero: outages render as "—" with a retry, never as $0.00; the DeFindex rate-limit backoff.
  • Wave 4 — automatic retries: one-shot silent retries for pure races (tx_bad_seq, nonce races), quote backoff, and flow polish.
