Decoded: the gate asked the server, got a genuinely fresh read (not floored), and that read contained the correct post-swap balances — 5 XLM gone, ~0.9 USDC arrived. The machinery I have been fixing for four rounds worked. It fetched the right numbers, put them in place, and ticked Done.

And your screen still showed the old numbers. Which means something overwrote them afterwards.

2 · The actual bug: last writer wins

When a swap finishes, the app fires an event (nf:activity-updated) so the activity feed updates immediately. The balance hook also listens to that event — and that hook is mounted in about 23 components (drawer, savings card, send modal, search bar, portfolio…). Every one of them fires its own balance refresh 800ms after the event.

800ms after a Stellar swap is before the ledger has settled (~5s). So all of those reads come back with pre-swap balances. Meanwhile the gate does its careful, ledger-aware read and gets the post-swap ones. Now there are two dozen answers in flight, one right and the rest stale — and the shared cache they all write into had a simple rule:

The rule that was wrong
Whichever fetch resolves last owns the screen. Not the freshest one — the slowest one. Your swap: the gate’s correct read landed (its own request took 5.4s), and a straggler carrying pre-swap numbers resolved after it and rewound the display. The 30-second background poll eventually fixed it — that is the “it updates later by itself” you kept seeing.

This also explains why five rounds of fixes behaved so confusingly: it is a race. Sometimes the fresh read resolved last and everything looked instant (“this worked for swaps”). Sometimes a stale one did, and it looked like nothing I fixed had helped. Rounds 1–4 each repaired a real defect in the refresh layer — and none of them could win a race that was decided after the finish line.

3 · The fix: age decides, not arrival order

Every balance payload already carries updatedAt — the server-side moment it was aggregated. Crucially, a cached/floored response keeps its original older timestamp, so stale data cannot masquerade as fresh. One pure rule now sits in front of every write:

pickNewerPayload(current, incoming):
  no current yet            → take incoming
  incoming has no timestamp → keep current   (undated must never beat dated)
  otherwise                 → the NEWER aggregation wins, ties go to incoming

Applied in all three places a payload can land:

  • the event-handler refresh (the herd of 23);
  • the gate’s own refreshFresh seed — which now also always hands the gate its own read for the changed/floored decision, whichever copy ends up on screen;
  • the localStorage snapshot, so a straggler cannot rewind what a fresh fetch already wrote to disk either.

7 new tests, including the exact case: an older aggregation arriving late is rejected.
