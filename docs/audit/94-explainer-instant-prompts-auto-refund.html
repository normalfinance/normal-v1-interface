Instant Prompts & Auto-Refund

Branch feat/swap-matrix-hardening, run 1 (2026-08-27) — doc 93 Phase 0a + 0b. What changed, why, and how to test it.

1 · "Approve in your wallet" now means the prompt is on screen (0a)

What you saw

After "Moving USDC from Lobstr" turned green, "Starting the Circle bridge" said Approve in your wallet — but nothing appeared for several seconds. You correctly guessed a hidden wait was living inside the wrong step.

What was actually happening

Interesting detail: step 1's checkmark was already honest — it only turns green once the moved USDC is verified visible on your Normal wallet. The hidden wait was after that: before Stellar can ask for your signature, the app must fetch your account, check the USDC allowance, and run a simulation of the transaction on the Soroban network (that's what catches errors before you sign). Those seconds of silent preparation were happening under a step whose label promised a signature prompt.

Now

stepwhat it covers
Moving USDC from Lobstrthe transfer and waiting until it is visible (unchanged)
Preparing the bridge transaction — NEW"A few seconds — no action needed yet": account fetch, allowance check, simulation
Starting the Circle bridgeflips active at the exact moment the signature prompt appears (a new onSigning callback fires right before the passkey/wallet popup)
The principle (now a standing rule)
Waiting is its own honest step. Any step that names a signature means the prompt is already on screen — for every engine, on the future run page too.

2 · No dead-end errors — the app makes the next move itself (0b)

Before

When a swap's exchange leg failed twice (original route + automatic failover), you got a red box and two buttons — the system knew there were no more automatic options, but made you figure that out and click "Bring back as USDC" yourself.

Now

  • When options are exhausted, the popup switches to the refund checklist by itself, with a blue explanation above the steps: "The exchange route kept failing — bringing your USDC back automatically."
  • Autopilot users: zero signatures. The refund's burn is a depositForBurn on Base — the same operation the autopilot signing policy already allows — so the refund now tries the server-side signer first. If that works, your USDC heads home without a single prompt.
  • Everyone else gets exactly one prompt ("Sending your USDC back — approve in your wallet") instead of a decision.
  • Manual "Bring back as USDC" still exists and also benefits from the signature-less path.
  • LI.FI direct swaps: the failure message no longer claims "no funds were moved" when the app cannot know that — it now says what happens next ("the bridge returns it automatically — check Activity").

One server-side change to know about

The autopilot burn endpoint used to require a source-swap transaction on the row (true for all real inbound swaps). Refund rows are pure USDC→USDC returns with no swap leg, so the endpoint now recognizes that exact shape — and its existing custody check (recipient must be one of YOUR wallets) still applies unchanged.
