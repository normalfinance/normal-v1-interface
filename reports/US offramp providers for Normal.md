# Add Ramp, keep Coinbase, stage MoonPay

Ramp Network is the only off-ramp surveyed that sells every asset Normal holds — BTC, ETH, SOL, XLM **and USDC on Stellar** — to a US bank account by ACH or RTP without the user opening an exchange account, and it exposes exactly the integration shape a Turnkey passkey wallet needs: a widget-emitted `SEND_CRYPTO` {address, amount} that the app signs and broadcasts itself, ECDSA-signed webhooks, and an official React Native SDK ([Ramp assets, Jul 2026](https://support.rampnetwork.com/en/articles/432-what-cryptoassets-does-ramp-network-support); [Ramp native flow](https://docs.rampnetwork.com/off-ramp-native-flow/integration)). It should become Normal's primary US rail, with the existing Coinbase Offramp kept as the zero-build fallback for Coinbase account holders and for the eight states Ramp excludes (New York, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Washington, Louisiana, Minnesota, Nevada, Vermont) ([Ramp supported states](https://support.rampnetwork.com/en/articles/433-supported-countries-territories-and-us-states-for-buying-and-selling-crypto)). MoonPay is the right second licensed ramp, not the first: it adds the 1% partner fee the team already negotiated, instant US rails (PayPal, Venmo, Cash App, Visa/Mastercard push-to-card) and EU/UK bank payout, but ACH takes 3–5 business days, its $3.99–$4.50 minimum fee makes sells under ~$100 expensive, and USDC on Stellar is not sellable there ([MoonPay payout methods](https://support.moonpay.com/customers/docs/all-supported-payment-methods); [MoonPay pricing disclosure](https://moonpay.com/legal/pricing_disclosure)). The stablecoin-API tier (Bridge, BlindPay, Crossmint) is attractive on paper for a USDC-first product but every one of them is sales-gated with unpublished fees, Crossmint's offramp API does not list Stellar at all, and none offers anything Ramp does not already deliver for Normal's current assets — so Bridge is a deferred option, not a launch dependency ([Crossmint create order](https://docs.crossmint.com/offramp/api-reference/create-order.md); [Bridge pricing](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/additional-information/pricing.md)). Transak, Onramper, Meld, Topper, Robinhood Connect, Kraken Embed, PayPal/Venmo, Cash App, Sphere, Coinflow, Beam and Stellar anchors each fail at least one hard requirement (US bank payout, no exchange account, Stellar, self-custody signing, or a partner API). The regulatory posture holds across the whole mix as long as the user's own key signs the send to the ramp's deposit address and the licensed ramp pays the user as principal; the one pattern to avoid is any provider model in which Normal nets its fee out of funds in transit or pays users from a Normal-owned account ([FinCEN FIN-2019-G001](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf)).

## Coinbase stays live but cannot be the primary rail

The integration Normal already ships is sound but structurally capped. Coinbase's own FAQ states that "a Coinbase account with linked bank details is required for Offramp and ACH withdrawals" and that "guest checkout is not supported for fiat withdrawal", which makes Coinbase KYC plus a linked US bank a hard funnel step before any first sell ([CDP FAQ](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/additional-resources/faq)). There is no partner-fee or revenue-share parameter anywhere in the sell URL or the Sell Quote API; Coinbase charges its own spread plus a method-dependent fee (the FAQ quotes **0.5% ACH** and 2.5% card from the onramp side) and describes the product as "free for developers", so Normal earns nothing on the Coinbase leg today ([CDP FAQ](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/additional-resources/faq.md)). The economics do improve for one asset: Coinbase advertises **0% fees on all USDC on/offramps** for "select apps upon request", a subsidy Normal should apply for, because it would make a Soroswap→CCTP→Base→Coinbase path for Stellar USDC cheaper at the ramp even though it adds two hops of fee and failure risk ([Coinbase zero-fee USDC](https://www.coinbase.com/developer-platform/discover/launches/zero-fee-usdc)).

Stellar is the open question. No Offramp doc names `stellar` as a `sellNetwork`, and the `OfframpTransaction` schema carries `to_address` but no memo or destination-tag field, so even if `/sell/options` for the US returns XLM there is no documented way to retrieve the memo a Stellar deposit to an exchange normally requires ([Offramp transaction status](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/offramp/transaction-status.md); [Offramp configurations](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/offramp/configurations.md)). Treat XLM and USDC-on-Stellar as unsupported on Coinbase until a `GET /onramp/v1/sell/options?country=US&subdivision=CA` call shows a `stellar` network entry and a real sell proves the funds land in the user's account rather than an unattributed hot wallet. Two cheap improvements are worth making regardless: Normal polls the sell-transactions API today, but CDP publishes `offramp.transaction.created/updated/success/failed` webhooks with at-least-once delivery and exponential-backoff retries, so status should move to webhook-first with polling as fallback; and the **30-minute** transaction timeout after "Cash out now" plus the single-use **5-minute** session token should drive a visible countdown in the send UI, since late funds "will still arrive in the users Coinbase account" but the Offramp order "will likely move to a TRANSACTION_STATUS_FAILED state" ([CDP webhooks](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/webhooks/onramp.md); [Offramp integration guide](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/offramp/offramp-integration-guide.md); [CDP FAQ](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/additional-resources/faq)). On mobile, Coinbase itself warns against WebViews because of WebAuthn limits and recommends Chrome Custom Tabs, `SFSafariViewController`/`ASWebAuthenticationSession`, or `react-native-inappbrowser-reborn`, with `redirectUrl` allow-listed in the CDP portal as either an `https://` origin or an exact `custom-scheme://path` ([Security requirements](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/security-requirements.md)).

## Ramp sells all five assets to a US bank with no exchange account

Ramp Network is the single provider whose primary documentation checks every box in the brief at once. Its July 2026 asset page lists, for USA users, BTC on Bitcoin, ETH on eight networks, SOL on Solana, "XLM (Stellar Lumens): Selling supported on Stellar network", and USDC selling on thirteen chains including **Stellar** ([Ramp assets](https://support.rampnetwork.com/en/articles/432-what-cryptoassets-does-ramp-network-support)). US payout is **ACH in up to 2 business days or RTP "as fast as 2 minutes"**, Europe gets SEPA and SEPA Instant ("as fast as 10 seconds"), and payouts "must be sent to a bank account or card owned by, and under the same name as, the user who makes the sale" — the first-party rule a non-custodial app wants ([Ramp payout methods](https://support.rampnetwork.com/en/articles/8992-what-payout-methods-does-ramp-support-for-selling-crypto)). The published sell fee is **0.99% with a $1.99 minimum** for both ACH and RTP, with the inbound network fee paid by the user from their own wallet; a $100 sell therefore costs the user about $1.99 at Ramp versus $3.99–$4.50 at MoonPay ([Ramp sell fees](https://support.rampnetwork.com/en/articles/8957-what-are-the-fees-for-selling-crypto)). Partner economics are negotiated rather than published — Ramp says "many partners benefit from fees lower than those listed on our website" and offers a "partner commission program" — so the fee-share number is a sales-call deliverable, not a documented figure ([Ramp partner pricing](https://support.rampnetwork.com/en/articles/31326-what-are-the-baseline-fees-for-integration-partners)).

The integration shape is the decisive advantage. Ramp's "native flow" is declared "required for mobile wallets": with `useSendCryptoCallback: true` the widget emits `{type: "SEND_CRYPTO", payload: {assetInfo, amount, address}}`, the host app builds, signs and broadcasts the transfer with the user's own key, and replies `{type: "SEND_CRYPTO_RESULT", payload: {txHash}}` ([Ramp off-ramp](https://docs.rampnetwork.com/off-ramp); [Ramp native flow](https://docs.rampnetwork.com/off-ramp-native-flow/integration)). That maps one-to-one onto Normal's Turnkey signing adapters for all four chains and avoids the copy-paste-an-address UX that Transak and Onramper fall back to. Webhooks fire `CREATED | RELEASED | EXPIRED` with the full `RampSale` payload (tx hash, crypto amount, fiat amount, payout method, fees), signed with ECDSA/sha256 in `X-Body-Signature` over a `fast-json-stable-stringify` body, retried four times at three-minute intervals ([Ramp webhooks](https://docs.rampnetwork.com/webhooks)). An official `@ramp-network/react-native-sdk` supports `enabledFlows: ['OFFRAMP']` and the send-crypto callback ([Ramp RN SDK](https://docs.rampnetwork.com/mobile/react-native-sdk)).

Three caveats must be verified before Ramp becomes the default. First, coverage: eight states are excluded (LA, MN, NV, NJ, NY, PA, VT, WA) and in Texas "stablecoins (and a few related assets) cannot be bought or sold", which removes USDC sells for Texans while leaving XLM/BTC/ETH/SOL — Normal should read Ramp's country/state gating from the widget rather than hardcode it ([Ramp states](https://support.rampnetwork.com/en/articles/433-supported-countries-territories-and-us-states-for-buying-and-selling-crypto); [Ramp assets](https://support.rampnetwork.com/en/articles/432-what-cryptoassets-does-ramp-network-support)). Second, the `SEND_CRYPTO` payload documents an `address` but **no memo field**, yet Ramp sells XLM and Stellar USDC; it must therefore either issue per-sale Stellar addresses or pass a memo in an undocumented way, and this has to be confirmed in the demo environment before a single Stellar sell ships ([Ramp native flow](https://docs.rampnetwork.com/off-ramp-native-flow/integration)). Third, the testing page lists deprecated testnets (Rinkeby, Mumbai, Ghostnet) and says nothing about off-ramp, and API keys come through a form or partner@ramp.network, so expect a human-in-the-loop onboarding and ask explicitly whether off-ramp can be exercised end to end in demo ([Ramp testing](https://docs.rampnetwork.com/testing-environment); [Ramp API keys](https://docs.rampnetwork.com/api-keys)).

## MoonPay buys instant rails, EU/UK and state coverage at a higher floor fee

MoonPay is the correct second ramp because it complements Ramp on exactly the axes Ramp is weak. Its payout table (updated August 26, 2026) lists US ACH at 3–5 business days alongside **instant** PayPal, Venmo and Cash App, Visa Direct and Mastercard Direct push-to-card (instant to one business day), SEPA/SEPA Instant for EUR and UK Faster Payments for GBP — the only provider in the survey with documented instant consumer rails in the US plus both EU and UK bank payout ([MoonPay payout methods](https://support.moonpay.com/customers/docs/all-supported-payment-methods)). The partner model is published: after KYB "you can add an additional affiliate fee to each order on top of the MoonPay fees", "typically 0.5-1.25%", configured via a Partner Success Manager and paid monthly in BTC/USDC/USDT once commissions reach $1,000; the fee appears on the sell transaction as `extraFeeAmount`, confirming it applies to sells ([MoonPay affiliate payouts](https://support.moonpay.com/customers/docs/how-do-affiliate-payouts-work); [MoonPay sell transaction](https://dev.moonpay.com/api-reference/widget/getselltransaction.md)). The team's planned 1% both sides sits inside that range. The cost is the user's floor: MoonPay's fee is "up to 4.5%" with "a minimum MoonPay fee of up to $3.99" (up to $4.50 when partner-referred), roughly 1% for bank transfers, plus an embedded spread — so a $100 sell pays about 4% to MoonPay before Normal's 1%, versus ~2% at Ramp ([MoonPay pricing disclosure](https://moonpay.com/legal/pricing_disclosure); [MoonPay sell XLM](https://www.moonpay.com/sell/xlm)). MoonPay sells are economical above roughly $400, which is also where Ramp's percentage fee dominates and the two converge.

On assets, MoonPay almost certainly sells XLM — it runs a dedicated "sell as little as $20 of XLM" page and its deposit flow supports `depositWalletAddressTag`, which "you must include with the deposit, otherwise the transaction will fail" — but **USDC on Stellar is not sellable**: MoonPay's `usdc` code is documented as "USD Coin (ERC-20)" and its sell expansion named USDC (Solana), not Stellar ([MoonPay sell XLM](https://www.moonpay.com/sell/xlm); [MoonPay off-ramp design](https://dev.moonpay.com/docs/design-your-off-ramp-integration); [MoonPay currencies](https://dev.moonpay.com/api-reference/widget/getcurrencies.md)). For a USDC-Stellar saver that means either a Soroswap hop to XLM before the MoonPay sell (adding Soroswap's fee and XLM price exposure during the quote window) or simply routing USDC-Stellar sells to Ramp. The self-custody mechanics are good: the SDK's `onInitiateDeposit` hands the app `transactionId`, `baseCurrencyCode`, `baseCurrencyAmount`, `depositWalletAddress` (and tag), the app signs with Turnkey and returns a deposit ID, and `refundWalletAddresses` in the signed URL guarantees any refund lands back in the user's own wallet ([MoonPay off-ramp design](https://dev.moonpay.com/docs/design-your-off-ramp-integration); [MoonPay off-ramp params](https://dev.moonpay.com/docs/ramps-sdk-sell-params)). Webhooks are `sell_transaction_created/updated/failed/requote_required`, HMAC-SHA256 in `Moonpay-Signature-V2`, at-least-once and unordered, 9 retries with a 5-second 2xx budget — the handler must enqueue and return ([MoonPay webhooks](https://dev.moonpay.com/api-reference/widget/webhooks.openapi.json); [MoonPay webhook overview](https://dev.moonpay.com/reference/reference-webhooks-overview)). `@moonpay/react-native-moonpay-sdk` has an explicit `flow: "sell"` ([MoonPay RN SDK](https://dev.moonpay.com/widget/off-ramp/integration-methods/sdks/react-native.md)).

Two uncertainties shape the timeline. MoonPay's dashboard — and therefore `pk_test_` keys — is described as available "following successful onboarding approval", and KYB "can vary ... from a few minutes to several days", so the sandbox (which does include a Stellar testnet) is probably not reachable before the KYB the team has not yet started ([MoonPay FAQ](https://dev.moonpay.com/docs/faq); [MoonPay sandbox](https://dev.moonpay.com/widget/sandbox-testing.md)). And the only state-exclusion list on record is the 2021 launch post (Hawaii, New York, Rhode Island); no current page confirms or retracts it, so MoonPay's value as a backstop for Ramp's eight excluded states is plausible for PA/NJ/WA/LA/MN/NV/VT but must be confirmed with Partner Success, and New York should be assumed closed on every ramp ([MoonPay ACH sell blog](https://www.moonpay.com/learn/blog/ach-sell)).

## Stablecoin APIs solve USDC-on-Stellar but trade product friction for a sales cycle

Only two API-first providers document native USDC-on-Stellar intake that ends in a US bank payout. Bridge (Stripe) lists Stellar among its source chains, returns a `blockchain_memo` for a Stellar liquidation address that "must be included in the crypto deposit memo", and pays out by ACH, FedNow or wire in USD plus SEPA and Faster Payments, with US minimums "mostly minimum of $1" ([Bridge payment routes](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/get-started/introduction/what-we-support/payment-routes); [Bridge liquidation address](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/orchestration/liquidation_address/liquidation_address)). A liquidation address is "a permanent payment route", so Bridge is the one provider that could give each Normal user a standing "cash-out address" rather than a per-sell quote, with ACH batched daily and real-time rails sent instantly ([Bridge offramp guide](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/get-started/guides/move-money/offramp_liquidation)). Bridge Building Inc (NMLS 2450917) is the licensed transmitter, the user accepts Bridge's ToS and completes Bridge's hosted KYC with decisions "typically less than one minute", and New York is absent from its license list ([Bridge fee disclosure](https://bridge.xyz/legal/fee-disclosure-statement/overview); [Bridge KYC links](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/customers/customers/kyclinks.md); [Bridge US licenses](https://www.bridge.xyz/legal/licenses/us-licenses-and-registrations)). The friction is commercial and compliance-shaped: pricing is "reach out to sales@bridge.xyz", the sandbox is not self-serve, and Bridge's partner revenue is a `custom_developer_fee_percent` that is "automatically withheld from the customer's transaction amount" and paid to Normal monthly — a fee netted out of the user's funds in transit, which is precisely the language the regulatory analysis says Normal should avoid, even though Bridge, not Normal, does the netting ([Bridge pricing](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/additional-information/pricing.md); [Bridge sandbox](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/get-started/introduction/quick-start/setting-up-sandbox.md); [Bridge developer fees](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/orchestration/fees-and-mins/devfees)). Bridge is therefore a Phase 3 option for a "USDC savings → bank" feature, to be taken to counsel first.

The rest of the tier is weaker for Normal. BlindPay supports Stellar only via an authorize→sign→payout-to-treasury call against a quote that "locks the exchange rate and fees for 5 minutes", explicitly allows payouts to third-party bank accounts (a B2B signal, not a consumer one), excludes Stellar from its managed offramp wallets, and costs $399–$1,599 per month before per-rail fees ([BlindPay payout Stellar](https://blindpay.com/docs/payout-stellar); [BlindPay payouts](https://blindpay.com/docs/payouts); [BlindPay pricing](https://blindpay.com/pricing)). Crossmint — the provider earlier internal notes assumed takes Stellar USDC natively — does not: its Create Offramp Order enum lists only `solana`, `base`, `polygon` in production with "additional chains available on request", and its chain matrix says "Stellar is supported in the EU and Rest of World, but not the US" ([Crossmint create order](https://docs.crossmint.com/offramp/api-reference/create-order.md); [Crossmint supported chains](https://docs.crossmint.com/introduction/supported-chains.md)). Crossmint does publish the best US rail table (ACH 1 business day and $50,000 per transaction, RTP instant and $1,000,000, wire no limit) and runs a fully simulated staging, but production needs "a signed Order Form and completed KYB verification" and fees are quoted per integration ([Crossmint payment methods](https://docs.crossmint.com/offramp/payment-methods.md); [Crossmint going to production](https://docs.crossmint.com/offramp/guides/going-to-production.md)). Zero Hash custodies USDC on Stellar and holds licenses in 51 US jurisdictions including a NY BitLicense, but it is a "Platform Operator" brokerage stack with 100–400 bps spreads, which would make Normal the account provider over custodied balances — the wrong side of the non-custodial line ([Zero Hash stablecoins](https://docs.zerohash.com/page/production-environment-stablecoins); [Zero Hash pricing](https://zerohash.com/disclosures/pricing-and-fees)). The Stellar-native route is closed: the Anchor Directory returns "No ramp assets available for this location" for the United States, and Beam no longer exists as a product after Modern Treasury's $40M acquisition ([Stellar Anchor Directory](https://anchors.stellar.org/?country=US); [The Block on Beam](https://www.theblock.co/post/375689/salesforce-backed-paytech-firm-modern-treasury-acquires-beam-stablecoin-startup-40-million-all-stock-fortune)).

## Routing by asset: four of five sell direct, one needs a decision

Because Ramp sells all five assets natively, the routing table collapses for the launch mix and hops become a fallback rather than a default. The team's internal routes (Soroswap XLM⇄USDC, LI.FI for BTC/ETH/SOL, CCTP Stellar⇄Base) remain useful only where a provider lacks an asset, and each hop adds a fee, a quote-window race and a failure mode.

| Asset | Ramp (primary) | Coinbase (fallback, account holders) | MoonPay (instant rails, EU/UK) |
|---|---|---|---|
| BTC | Direct, ACH/RTP ([assets](https://support.rampnetwork.com/en/articles/432-what-cryptoassets-does-ramp-network-support)) | Direct (from_address not validated for BTC) ([guide](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/offramp/offramp-integration-guide.md)) | Direct (launch asset) ([blog](https://www.moonpay.com/learn/blog/ach-sell)) |
| ETH | Direct | Direct | Direct |
| SOL | Direct | Direct (Solana listed L1) ([welcome](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/introduction/welcome.md)) | Direct (verify `isSellSupported`) |
| XLM | Direct — verify memo handling in demo | Unverified; no memo field in schema — treat as unsupported | Likely direct with `depositWalletAddressTag` as memo ([design](https://dev.moonpay.com/docs/design-your-off-ramp-integration)) |
| USDC on Stellar | Direct (not in Texas) | Hop: CCTP→Base, then sell USDC-Base (apply for 0% USDC program) | Not sellable; hop Soroswap→XLM then sell XLM, or route to Ramp |

Two rules follow. USDC-on-Stellar sells should default to Ramp and never be hopped to MoonPay, since a Soroswap swap into XLM exposes the user to XLM price movement inside MoonPay's quote window and pays a second fee for no rail advantage. And XLM itself should ship on Ramp first only after the memo question is closed in demo; if Ramp turns out to issue per-sale Stellar addresses without memos, Normal's Stellar send adapter still needs memo support for MoonPay, so the memo field belongs in the sell send UI from day one.

## Compliance: the pattern that keeps Normal a software provider

The whole provider mix is compatible with Normal's non-custodial posture if one invariant holds end to end: the user's Turnkey passkey signs a transfer from the user's own address to a deposit address controlled by the licensed ramp, and the ramp — as principal — pays fiat to the user's own bank. FinCEN's 2019 CVC guidance treats unhosted-wallet software as outside money transmission when the user transacts for themselves and the provider "does not control funds any more than an operating system or a mobile device does", a reading Coin Center and law-firm summaries confirm ([FinCEN FIN-2019-G001](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf); [Coin Center](https://coincenter.org/fincens-new-cryptocurrency-guidance-matches-coin-center-recommendations/)). Ramp's US subsidiary is FinCEN-registered with state MTLs, MoonPay sells "as a principal trader" authorized "to send payments directly to our users", and Bridge and Crossmint name their licensed US entities — so in every recommended flow the regulated party is the ramp ([Ramp MTLs](https://rampnetwork.com/blog/ramp-network-6-new-mtl-licenses); [MoonPay ACH sell](https://www.moonpay.com/learn/blog/ach-sell); [Crossmint offramps](https://www.crossmint.com/products/offramps)). Fee share is defensible as marketing compensation: Transak describes the industry pattern as "a percentage charged on the transaction amount and paid to the wallet rather than to the provider", and California's DFPI has an opinion titled "Referral of customers to financial institutions not money transmission" ([Transak blog](https://transak.com/blog/how-on-ramps-make-wallets-money); [CA DFPI OP 8247](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/337/2022/03/OP8247-2-22-22-Referral-of-customers-to-financial-institutions-not-money-transmission.pdf)).

The federal backdrop has worsened, not improved. Section 604 of the CLARITY Act would have codified that a "non-controlling developer or provider shall not be treated as a money transmitting business", but Senate cloture **failed 49–50 on 2026-09-15** and passage this Congress "is now widely viewed as unlikely", so the 2019 guidance plus state law governs ([Orrick, Oct 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/10/The-CLARITY-Act-Stalls-in-the-Senate-Whats-Next-for-Digital-Asset-Regulation); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/15/senate-cloture-vote-on-clarity-act-fails-dealing-regulatory-setback-to-crypto-industry.html)). Pennsylvania's Act 7 of 2025 now covers virtual currency with a self-hosted-wallet exemption that does not extend to "those using self-hosted wallets to transmit virtual currency on behalf of others for compensation" — a direct description of what Normal must not become ([PA DoBS](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dobs/newsroom/dobs-regulating-virtual-currency-transmitters-thanks-to-new-bipa); [Orrick InfoBytes](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-07-09/pennsylvania-enacts-new-law-regulating-cryptoasset-transactions/)). Three concrete design consequences: never route a sell through a Normal-controlled relayer, escrow or omnibus address before the ramp (the existing CCTP relayer and any company-paid gas pattern should be reviewed against the same test); never present Normal's fee as deducted from the payout — accept it as a ramp-paid affiliate commission, which argues for Ramp/MoonPay-style models over Bridge's withheld developer fee; and geo-gate each sell using the ramp's own state list, with New York treated as closed everywhere since Ramp, MoonPay, Bridge and Robinhood all exclude it ([Ramp states](https://support.rampnetwork.com/en/articles/433-supported-countries-territories-and-us-states-for-buying-and-selling-crypto); [Bridge licenses](https://www.bridge.xyz/legal/licenses/us-licenses-and-registrations)). For EU users, route only to MiCA-authorized ramps (MoonPay, Ramp, Crossmint Europe's CNMV CASP registration, or Onramp.money per the team's separate evaluation); this carries no US licensing consequence.

## Recommended mix and build order

The recommended mix is **Ramp as the primary US rail, Coinbase retained as the account-holder fallback, MoonPay as the instant-rail and EU/UK layer, Onramp.money's whitelabel API as the non-US complement for EUR/GBP/TRY/MXN/BRL/INR, and Bridge held in reserve for a USDC-only standing cash-out address**. The order is driven by what unblocks the most users soonest with the least sales-cycle risk.

Phase 0 (this week, no new vendor) hardens what is live. Call Coinbase `GET /onramp/v1/sell/options?country=US&subdivision=CA` and grep for `stellar`; apply to the USDC zero-fee program; register the four `offramp.transaction.*` webhook event types via the CDP CLI and demote polling to fallback; add the 30-minute countdown and a Chrome Custom Tabs/`ASWebAuthenticationSession` path on mobile ([Offramp configurations](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/offramp/configurations.md); [CDP webhooks](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/webhooks/onramp.md)). Phase 1 (start now, 4–8 weeks to production) is Ramp: submit the API-key form and partner@ramp.network outreach in parallel with assembling one reusable KYB pack — formation documents, good standing, UBO chart, director IDs and proof of address, the set Turnkey says onramp providers typically request with a **1–4 business day** review "or longer" ([Turnkey fiat on-ramp](https://docs.turnkey.com/wallets/fiat-on-ramp)). Build the native flow once (web SDK `onSendCrypto`, RN SDK callback) over the existing per-chain Turnkey send adapters, verify the ECDSA webhook against Ramp's published demo key, and run the Stellar memo test as a gate before enabling XLM or USDC-Stellar; negotiate the partner commission in the same conversation. Phase 2 (begin KYB immediately, integrate after Ramp ships) is MoonPay: start the KYB the team planned for August 2026 now because test keys appear to be gated behind "onboarding approval"; integrate `onInitiateDeposit` with `refundWalletAddresses` and `externalTransactionId`, configure the 1% affiliate fee with Partner Success, confirm the current US state exclusions and whether Venmo/Cash App payouts are exposed in the partner widget, and offer MoonPay in the UI as "instant to PayPal/Venmo/card" and as the EU/UK bank option rather than as a second ACH button ([MoonPay affiliate payouts](https://support.moonpay.com/customers/docs/how-do-affiliate-payouts-work); [MoonPay sandbox](https://dev.moonpay.com/widget/sandbox-testing.md)). Phase 2b is the Onramp.money whitelabel integration for non-US fiat, which the team has already scoped. Phase 3 (only if USDC-savings cash-out volume justifies a sales cycle) is Bridge: counsel review of the withheld-developer-fee model first, then a support-approved sandbox account, then one Stellar liquidation address per user per bank account with the `blockchain_memo` surfaced in the send UI ([Bridge liquidation address](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/orchestration/liquidation_address/liquidation_address)). Meld becomes worth revisiting only once Normal runs three or more ramps and wants a single routing/quote layer, and only after confirming whether Meld brokers provider KYB for small partners ([Meld provider setup](https://docs.meld.io/docs/stablecoins/for-all-products/service-provider-setup.md)).

The sell UX should be provider-agnostic from the first line of code: a quote with a visible expiry (Coinbase 30 minutes, MoonPay `quoteExpiresAt`, Ramp's sale `EXPIRED` state), a deposit address plus optional memo pre-filled into the Turnkey signing flow and never hand-typed, an amount locked to the quote, the user's own same-chain address always passed as refund address, and a five-state progress list — awaiting send, sent with hash, provider confirming, fiat initiated, settled with the rail's own timing ([Offramp integration guide](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/offramp/offramp-integration-guide.md); [MoonPay sell transaction](https://dev.moonpay.com/api-reference/widget/getselltransaction.md); [Ramp SDK reference](https://docs.rampnetwork.com/sdk-reference)). Webhook handlers for all three ramps share one shape: verify signature, reject stale timestamps, enqueue, return 2xx, reconcile idempotently on provider transaction id plus status.

## Conclusion

The survey overturns two assumptions the team carried into it. The first is that Stellar USDC off-ramp would need either a stablecoin-API specialist or an internal bridge hop; in fact a mainstream licensed ramp, Ramp Network, already lists USDC on Stellar as sellable for US users with ACH and RTP, which makes the CCTP and Soroswap legs fallbacks rather than prerequisites and removes the strongest argument for taking on a sales-gated API vendor at launch. The second is that Crossmint was the natural Stellar-native off-ramp; its current offramp API lists Solana, Base and Polygon only, and its own chain table excludes Stellar for US users, so that path should be dropped from planning until Crossmint confirms Stellar in writing.

What remains genuinely open is small and testable: whether Ramp carries a Stellar memo in its `SEND_CRYPTO` handshake, whether Coinbase's `/sell/options` returns a `stellar` network for the US, which states MoonPay excludes today, and the partner commission percentages Ramp and Coinbase will not publish. Every one of those resolves with one API call or one sales conversation, not with more desk research — which is the signal to stop researching and start the Ramp application. The legislative setback of September 2026 changes none of the recommended build; it only raises the cost of ever drifting from the "user signs, ramp pays" invariant that every phase above preserves.
