# Exchange-Connect / Big-Tech Off-Ramps and US Regulatory Constraints for a Non-Custodial Wallet (as of 2026-10-08)

Scope: Part A = "exchange-connect" and big-tech sell flows a self-custody wallet (web + React Native) can embed to let US users sell BTC / ETH / SOL / XLM / USDC-on-Stellar to a US bank. Part B = money-transmitter and KYB constraints for embedding licensed third-party ramps. Research, not legal advice. Items marked **[older]** are from pre-2025 sources and may be stale.

## Key Question 1 — Does any big-tech or exchange platform offer a no-account or account-linked sell flow a wallet can embed in 2026, and on what terms?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only **account-linked** flows exist among exchanges/big-tech: Robinhood Connect (user must have/create a Robinhood account; Stellar incl. memo is supported in a public partner implementation) and Coinbase Offramp (Coinbase-hosted sell URL, ACH or Coinbase balance payout). No "no-account" sell exists from PayPal/Venmo, Cash App, Gemini, Kraken or Stripe for third-party self-custody wallets; the only true no-prior-account (KYC-in-widget) sells are the licensed ramp widgets (Topper, MoonPay, etc.), and Topper pays to debit cards, not bank accounts.

Off-ramp: "no deposit limits; selling converts to buying power with no fees". — [Robinhood Connect support article](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/robinhood-connect)
- US coverage: all US states except New York; North Carolina, Hawaii and Puerto Rico residents must maintain a brokerage account; NC residents can only use Robinhood buying power. — [Robinhood Connect support article](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/robinhood-connect)

Transaction **times out 30 minutes** after initiation. Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana L1s and Base/Optimism/Arbitrum L2s; **Stellar is not explicitly named** in the docs excerpt. — [Coinbase Onramp welcome](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/introduction/welcome.md)

## Key Question 2 — What must Normal (non-custodial, Turnkey passkey wallets) avoid to stay out of money-transmitter status when offering sells?

### Takeaway
Under FinCEN's 2019 CVC guidance, supplying unhosted-wallet software and routing a user to a licensed ramp that transacts directly with the user is not money transmission; Normal becomes a transmitter the moment it accepts the user's crypto or pays the user fiat from its own account (e.g., a Normal-owned Circle Mint or Bridge virtual account). Federal legislative codification (BRCA, Section 604 of the CLARITY Act) failed Senate cloture 49–50 on 2026-09-15, so the 2019 guidance plus a patchwork of state laws (now including Pennsylvania's 2025 virtual-currency MTA expansion) remains the operative framework.

### Cited Findings
- FinCEN FIN-2019-G001 ("Application of FinCEN's Regulations to Certain Business Models Involving Convertible Virtual Currencies"), issued 2019-05-09, applicable to MSBs. — [FinCEN guidance landing page](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/guidance/application-fincens-regulations-certain-business-models); [PDF](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf)
- The guidance distinguishes hosted wallets ("third-party control of user funds") from unhosted wallets ("leave control with the user"), and applies an activity-based money-transmission analysis to CVC kiosks, payment processors, DApps, trading platforms, P2P exchangers and ICO models. — [CryptoSlate FinCEN 2019 law profile](https://cryptoslate.com/crypto-laws/fincen-2019-convertible-virtual-currency-guidance-usa/)
- Use of unhosted wallets ("software a user has on their own computer or device that allows them to interact directly with the payment system") "does not make a person a money transmitter if a third party is not necessary for the transactions to occur" and the person is transacting for themselves; software developers "are not money transmitters unless they use the software to engage in a money transmission business". FinCEN regulations exempt persons providing only "the delivery, communication, or network access services used by a money transmitter to support money transmission services"; suppliers of tools "are engaged in trade and not money transmission". — [O'Melveny alert on FIN-2019-G001](https://www.omm.com/resources/alerts-and-publications/alerts/fincen-speaks-crypto-new-guidance/) and [CryptoSlate profile](https://cryptoslate.com/crypto-laws/fincen-2019-convertible-virtual-currency-guidance-usa/) (language as surfaced in search excerpts; the O'Melveny page returned 403 on full fetch)
- Coin Center: the 2019 guidance confirms "software wallet providers, multi-sig providers, decentralized exchanges, and other non-custodial services are not regulated as money transmitters"; noncustodial wallet providers "do not control funds any more than an operating system or a mobile device does". — [Coin Center](https://coincenter.org/fincens-new-cryptocurrency-guidance-matches-coin-center-recommendations/) (search excerpt; direct fetch failed on TLS)
- Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act (BRCA), reintroduced Jan 2026 by Sens. Lummis and Wyden and embedded as **Section 604 of the CLARITY Act**: a "non-controlling developer or provider shall not be treated as a money transmitting business" under the BSA solely for creating/publishing distributed-ledger software, providing self-custody hardware or software, or infrastructure support; the standard turns on inability to move or freeze user funds or hold private keys for users. — [The Block: Wyden urges Senate to preserve developer protections](https://theblock.co/post/407648/wyden-urges-senate-leaders-preserve-contested-blockchain-developer-protections-in-broader-crypto-bill); [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/solana-policy-institute-ceo-urges-senate-to-keep-open-source-developer-shields-in-clarity-act); [Autheo blog (secondary)](https://www.autheo.com/blog/clarity-act-validator-node-operator-protections-2026)
- **2026-09-15: Senate cloture on the CLARITY Act (H.R. 3633) failed 49–50**, 11 short of 60; "passage during this Congress is now widely viewed as unlikely"; Sen. Tillis voted no to preserve reconsideration; obstacles were ethics provisions on officials' crypto holdings and stablecoin yield. State money-transmitter and virtual-currency licensing regimes "will continue to govern operations". Interim federal framework = SEC/CFTC March 2026 Joint Interpretation (taxonomy). — [Orrick, Oct 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/10/The-CLARITY-Act-Stalls-in-the-Senate-Whats-Next-for-Digital-Asset-Regulation); [CNBC 2026-09-15](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/15/senate-cloture-vote-on-clarity-act-fails-dealing-regulatory-setback-to-crypto-industry.html); [Senate Daily Press 2026-09-15](https://www.dailypress.senate.gov/tuesday-september-15-2026/)
- Pennsylvania Act 7 of 2025 (SB 202), signed 2025-06-27, effective 60 days later: extends the PA Money Transmitter Act to virtual currency; entities facilitating virtual-currency transfers for compensation must be licensed like traditional transmitters. — [PA DoBS newsroom](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dobs/newsroom/dobs-regulating-virtual-currency-transmitters-thanks-to-new-bipa)
- The PA law exempts "self-hosted wallets in which the user has direct control of the records and makes transactions using that wallet on their own", but "those using self-hosted wallets to transmit virtual currency on behalf of others for compensation are subject to licensure". — [Orrick InfoBytes 2025-07-09](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-07-09/pennsylvania-enacts-new-law-regulating-cryptoasset-transactions/); [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/pennsylvania-to-require-licenses-for-virtual-currency-transmitters)
- Texas Department of Banking: requires "an individualized analysis of an entity's handling of the fiat currency" to determine whether regulation applies; approaches vary by state and the question is "not yet settled". — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/financial-services-law/pennsylvania-joins-other-states-with-guidance-on-v)
- Fee-share / referral treatment: California DFPI opinion OP 8247 (2022-02-22) titled "Referral of customers to financial institutions not money transmission". — [CA DFPI OP 8247 (PDF)](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/337/2022/03/OP8247-2-22-22-Referral-of-customers-to-financial-institutions-not-money-transmission.pdf) (title from search; PDF fetch returned 403)
- Acting as a referral service for a financial institution may not require an MTL if the company "does not sell or issue payment instruments, issue stored value, receive money for transmission, and never holds any type of fiat or cryptocurrency"; 25–35 states have an agent-of-the-payee exemption. — [Troutman Pepper money-transmission podcast transcript (PDF)](https://www.troutman.com/a/web/317708/82Hgxd/transcript_2022023_ce_s01ep02_money-transmission_final.pdf) (secondary, 2022 **[older]**)
- How ramps pay wallets: "Wallets earn a partner fee, which is a percentage charged on the transaction amount and paid to the wallet rather than to the provider, set on top of the provider's own fee." — [Transak blog: How on-ramps make wallets money](https://transak.com/blog/how-on-ramps-make-wallets-money)
- Licensed ramps carry the licenses: Ramp Network's US subsidiary registered as an MSB with FinCEN (38 states) and later added MTLs in AL, AK, MD, NM, ND, OH "allowing direct service without third-party intermediaries". — [Ramp blog: 6 new MTL licenses](https://rampnetwork.com/blog/ramp-network-6-new-mtl-licenses); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ramp-expands-presence-in-us-with-fincen-regulation)
- MoonPay sells in the US "as a principal trader" and sends ACH payments directly to users. — [MoonPay blog](https://www.moonpay.com/learn/blog/ach-sell)
- Bridge virtual accounts are "permanent, reusable fiat deposit addresses that convert incoming fiat into crypto and deliver it to a specified destination" with US account/routing numbers; Bridge itself holds state MTLs (NMLS 2450917). — [Bridge API docs: virtual accounts](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/orchestration/virtual_accounts/virtual-account); [Bridge US licenses](https://www.bridge.xyz/legal/licenses/us-licenses-and-registrations)

### Inferences
- **Stay-safe pattern for Normal:** (1) the user's Turnkey passkey wallet signs and broadcasts the crypto send directly to the ramp's deposit address (Coinbase `to_address`, MoonPay per-order address, Topper order address); (2) fiat is paid by the licensed ramp (as principal) to the user's bank/card/exchange balance; (3) Normal never holds crypto or fiat and never operates a pooled/omnibus address; (4) Normal's revenue is a partner/referral fee paid by the ramp, consistent with the Transak model and the CA DFPI referral opinion.
- **What flips Normal into transmitter status:** receiving user crypto into a Normal-controlled address (hot wallet, relayer, or smart-contract escrow Normal can move) before forwarding to the ramp; paying users USD from a Normal-owned Circle Mint or Bridge virtual/customer account (Normal would be "receiving value from one person and transmitting it to another"); or charging the user a fee for a transfer Normal executes on their behalf (the PA "on behalf of others for compensation" trigger). The CCTP relayer and any "Normal pays gas from its own account" patterns in the existing codebase should be reviewed against the same test — they are closer to the line than a pure redirect.
- **Fee-share revenue:** a referral/partner fee from a licensed ramp is compensation for marketing/distribution, not for transmitting value, and should be structured and documented that way (flat or % referral paid by the ramp to Normal, disclosed to users; Normal never nets its fee out of user funds in transit). Avoid "Normal fee deducted from payout" language in UI/contracts.
- **State patchwork:** with BRCA dead for this Congress, expect state-by-state variance (PA now explicitly covers VC transmission with a self-hosted exemption; NY BitLicense excludes most ramps — Robinhood Connect, MoonPay sell, Bridge all exclude NY). Normal should geo-gate sells per ramp's own state list rather than its own.
- MiCA (brief): EU ramps act as CASPs; Normal's US-first posture means EU users should be routed only to MiCA-authorized ramps; no US licensing consequence.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full FIN-2019-G001 text to quote the four hosted/unhosted factors (owner of value, storage location, control, initiation) or the payment-processor exemption's four conditions verbatim; cited via law-firm/industry summaries. Report writer should cite the FinCEN PDF as primary and treat quotes as paraphrase.
- No 2025–2026 FinCEN statement specifically on non-custodial wallets embedding third-party ramps was found; the field is governed by the 2019 guidance plus state law.
- CA DFPI OP 8247 facts could not be read (403); only the title confirms the conclusion.
- No source found that specifically analyzes Circle Mint / Bridge "pay user from own account" as transmission; this is inference from the definitional test.

## Key Question 3 — Which KYB documents and timelines should the founder plan for across providers, and what is best-practice UX for self-custody sells?

### Takeaway
Plan for a standard corporate KYB pack (formation docs, registration/good standing, ownership/UBO chart, IDs + proof of address for directors/UBOs) and 1–4 business days of compliance review per ramp for the fast path, with "or longer" explicitly warned by Turnkey; exchange-connect programs (Robinhood, Kraken) are sales-led with no published timelines. UX must be built around short, hard validity windows (Coinbase: 5-min session token, 30-min transaction timeout; MoonPay: 20-min fixed price), one-time deposit addresses with memos, a mandatory refund address, and polling-based status.

You can expect review to take 1-4 business days (or longer) depending on document availability and follow-up questions." - Coinbase Offramp: single-use session token, 5-minute expiry; transaction times out 30 minutes after the user initiates in the widget; deposit address only revealed via the Transaction Status API (`to_address`); partner must pass the user's funded wallet address; `redirectUrl` returns the user to the app. — [Coinbase Offramp integration guide](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/onramp/offramp/offramp-integration-guide)
- MoonPay: fixed, guaranteed price for twenty minutes from order creation; unique deposit address per sell order, never reuse; ACH credit 3–10 business days. — [MoonPay blog](https://www.moonpay.com/learn/blog/ach-sell); [MoonPay deposit address article](https://support.moonpay.com/en/articles/700869-how-to-find-your-deposit-address-for-a-sell-order); [MoonPay ACH FAQ](https://support.moonpay.com/en/articles/384611-automated-clearing-house-ach-faqs)
- Topper: refund crypto address required in the bootstrap token with optional memo/destination tag; wallet send URL deep-link with order amount/address placeholders; events/webhooks documented separately; partner fee configurable. — [Topper docs: crypto off-ramp](https://docs.topperpay.com/flows/crypto-offramp)
- Robinhood Connect (partner implementation): status by polling Order Details API (10 attempts, 12-second intervals); Stellar flagged as memo-required. — [endaoment POC README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/endaoment/robinhood-connect-poc/main/robinhood-onramp/README.md)
- Venmo/PayPal: "Some networks require additional information like a destination tag or memo" and sender and recipient must select the same network. — [Venmo Help](https://help.venmo.com/cs/articles/crypto-transfers-vhel232)
- Venly (aggregating Transak/MoonPay/Ramp off-ramp URLs): on fiat transfer failure "crypto refunds are automatically processed... in 5 minutes". — [Venly docs: fiat off-ramps](https://docs.venly.io/docs/fiat-off-ramps) (aggregator doc; provider-specific SLA not verified)

### Inferences
- **Founder's KYB pack (prepare once, reuse):** certificate of incorporation/formation; bylaws or operating agreement; certificate of good standing; EIN letter; cap table / UBO chart with ≥25% owners identified; passport/driver's licence + proof of address for each director and UBO; business address proof; AML/compliance policy summary and product description (how funds flow, who holds what); website/app URLs and privacy/ToS; bank account proof. This matches the Turnkey list and standard MSB-partner practice. Lead time: 1–4 business days for self-serve ramps (MoonPay/Coinbase CDP/Topper-type), realistically 2–6 weeks for sales-led exchange programs (Robinhood Connect, Kraken Embed) given contract negotiation — the latter is an estimate, not a sourced figure.
- **Sell UX rules derived from the docs:** (1) Fetch quote → show countdown matching the provider window (Coinbase 30 min, MoonPay 20 min); disable "Send" when expired and re-quote. (2) Pre-fill the exact `to_address` + memo (Stellar/XRP) from the provider API into the Turnkey signing flow; never let the user hand-type. (3) Lock amount to the quoted amount; underpayment/overpayment behaviour is provider-specific — Topper requires a refund address so the ramp can return mismatched sends; Normal should always pass the user's own wallet address (same chain) as refund address. (4) Show in-flight states: "awaiting your send" → "sent (tx hash)" → "provider confirming" → "fiat initiated" → "settled (ACH 3–10 business days)". (5) Status via polling where no webhooks exist (Robinhood Connect) and via webhooks where offered (Topper events; Coinbase Transaction Status API). (6) Chargebacks: crypto sends are irreversible and the fiat leg is a push credit from the licensed ramp, so Normal carries no chargeback exposure on sells — but the wallet should warn that a send after the window expires may be refunded minus network fees rather than paid out.
- Because Coinbase requires the funded wallet `addresses` param and MoonPay issues per-order addresses, Normal's per-asset lazy Turnkey address model is compatible; the Stellar memo requirement means Normal's send UI must support memo text for sells even if Normal's own receive flow does not.

### Gaps
- No provider page listing the exact documents demanded for off-ramp (vs on-ramp) partner KYB; the Turnkey list is the only consolidated primary source found.
- No published 2026 KYB SLA from MoonPay, Transak, Ramp, Crossmint or Bridge was retrievable (MoonPay partner pages exist but could not be fetched).
- Provider-specific over/under-payment rules (tolerances, refund fees, time to refund) were not found for Coinbase Offramp, MoonPay or Robinhood Connect.
- Deposit-address validity windows for Topper and Robinhood Connect were not stated in fetched pages.
